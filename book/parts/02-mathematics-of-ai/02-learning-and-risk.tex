\chapter{Learning, Loss, and Risk}
\label{ch:learning-and-risk}

Desired behaviour is reduced to a scalar. Population risk $\E[\ell(f(X),Y)]$ is the quantity we care about; empirical risk on a finite sample $\data$ is what we can compute.

The gap between the two is the first statistical problem of learning. Assumptions about the data-generating process must stay explicit.
